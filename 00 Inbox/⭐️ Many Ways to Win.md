# Many ways to win

<div align="center">
  <img src="https://pbs.twimg.com/profile_images/1563635599906549761/wIum4L8I.jpg" width="220" />
</div>

- Why do people flock to something? What creates an edge, an advantage, a moat? How do things win, or at least survive, in a highly competitive landscape?
- Successful things tend to optimize competency on a narrow set of strategies, often a unique and unusual combination of strategies
- Here are eighty such strategies, grouped in broad categories

### Accumulation

- **Usership:** a critical mass of users, network effect. Examples: Phone system, the internet, scaled social networks.
- **Completeness:** all-in-one tool, one-stop shop. Examples: Swiss army knife, Walmart.
- **Aggregation:** an environment or marketplace designed for broad participation. Examples: Cities, malls, Amazon, Craigslist.
- **Diversification:** low-dependency on any one stream of resources. Examples: Conglomerates, omnivores.

### Price

- **Affordability:** comparatively low-cost. Examples: Mass production, fast food, fast fashion.
- **Luxury:** intentionally high-cost, often labor-intensive, or status symbol. Examples: Veblen goods, supercars, luxury watches.
- **Skimming:** ingests a large volume of very small transactions or resources. Examples: Credit cards, baleen whales, planktivores.

### Time

- **Craftsmanship:** uses more time, precision, and attention to detail than competitors. Examples: Handmade watches, bespoke tailoring, Leica.
- **Endurance:** ability to operate continuously over a long period of time. Examples: Marathon runners, nuclear submarines.

### Uniqueness

- **Specialization:** strong competence in a narrow niche. Examples: Trade schools, pandas.
- **Versatility:** general competence adapted to many tasks. Examples: CPUs, opposable thumbs.
- **Hybrid:** competence in two distinct areas, or blending of two strengths not usually found together. Examples: Frogs, cronuts, amphibious vehicles, hybrid cars.
- **Divergence:** creativity, unusualness, differentiation from the norm
- **Rarity:** naturally limited in quantity, at the edge of the distribution curve. Examples: Beachfront property, Victor Wembanyama.
- **Scarcity:** artificially limited in quantity. Examples: Collectibles, NFTs.

### Offense

- **Litigation:** weakens opponents through legal burden. Examples: Patent trolls, lawsuits, Mickey Mouse.
- **Parasitism:** extracts or hijacks resources from a host. Examples: Mosquitos, tapeworm.
- **Espionage:** acquires enemy secrets. Examples: Spies.
- **Swarming:** concentrated attack from a mass of individually weak agents. Examples: Bees, DDOS attacks, Zerg rush.

### Defense

- **Reliability:** reduces maintenance costs, minimizes downtime. Examples: Zippo lighters, Toyota cars.
- **Predictability:** repeatable process with little deviation to create a consistent product or experience. Examples: Disneyland, Starbucks.
- **Unpredictability:** creating an element of surprise, confusion, or variability. Examples: Squid ink, smoke screen, Banksy, Lady Gaga.
- **Decentralization:** redundancy and distributed competency to reduce single points of failure. Examples: Solar power, grassroots movements.
- **Privacy:** protection against unwanted information disclosure. Examples: Encrypted messaging apps, VPNs.
- **Durability:** physical robustness, resistance to damage, or injury. Examples: LeBron James, cast iron skillets.
- **Neutrality:** displays long-term non-belligerence, a safe haven. Examples: Switzerland, Red Cross.
- **Obscurity:** survival by remaining unknown or undetected. Examples: Deep-sea creatures, closed-source software.
- **Antifragility:** becomes stronger when exposed to stress or usage. Examples: BitTorrent, muscles.

### Deception

- **Camouflage:** ability to blend into the surrounding environment. Examples: Chameleons, stealth aircraft.
- **Mimicry:** superficially adopts characteristics of another species to trick opponents. Examples: Harmless snakes mimicking venomous ones.
- **Decoy:** distracts or misleads adversaries. Examples: Chaff, fake security cameras.

### Timing

- **First-mover:** acts first to gain an advantage. Examples: Netflix, Tesla.
- **Second-mover:** fast-follower, moves quickly to copy the first-mover. Examples: Facebook-Snapchat.
- **Last-mover:** waits until all opponents have expended energy on failed approaches. Examples: Apple.

### Accreditation

- **Monopoly:** artificial control of a resource or market approved by the governing body. Examples: Patents, regulatory capture.
- **Prestige:** endorsed by authoritative sources via awards, degrees, or other recognition. Examples: Michelin-starred restaurants, Ivy League graduates.
- **Curation:** selectiveness, particular ability in choosing and grouping. Examples: Art galleries, HBO, venture capital.

### Collaboration

- **Union:** reduce friction between potential competitors by adopting a common set of rules. Examples: Mergers and acquisitions, United States, European Union.
- **Alliance:** partnership that enables some benefits of a union while maintaining independence. Examples: Partnerships.
- **Centralization:** single decision-making entity. Examples: Dictatorships.
- **Standardization:** emergent alignment that reduces friction. Examples: Language, railway gauge, metric system.
- **Symbiosis:** mutually beneficial dependency between two organisms. Examples: Two-party political system, fig wasp.
- **Distributed ownership:** owned by the community. Examples: REI Co-op, Green Bay Packers.

### Speed and scale

- **Iteration:** ability to iterate and change quickly, shortening the cycle time between improvements. Examples: SpaceX.
- **Efficiency:** uses a low amount of energy or resources to perform similar capabilities to competitors. Examples: LED bulbs.
- **Agility:** nimbleness, easily adapts to a changing environment. Examples: Gymnasts, squirrels, go-karts.
- **Precision:** high level of accuracy and exactness in performance or output. Examples: Swiss watches, CNC machining.
- **Blitzing:** sudden attack, expending intense resources in a concentrated manner. Examples: Hostile takeovers, blitz marketing campaigns.
- **Composability:** ability to assemble components and systems in different configurations. Examples: APIs, Stripe.
- **Modularity:** composed of independent units that can be used in different combinations. Examples: LEGO, prefab buildings.

### Ease

- **Intuitiveness:** ease of use and understanding, without explicit instruction. Examples: Dieter Rams designs.
- **Fun:** provides enjoyment and amusement. Examples: Video games, amusement parks, Duolingo.
- **Simplicity:** limited points of failure, minimalistic design. Examples: Hammer, MUJI.
- **Low-friction:** minimal resistance or hassle in use or interaction. Examples: One-click ordering.

### Transformation

- **Malleability:** ability to change appearance or form, can be modified or customized easily. Examples: Clay, software code, method actors.
- **Metamorphosis:** transforms between states optimized for different functions or environments. Examples: Caterpillars → butterflies, tadpole → frogs.
