- thinking about what sets apart my coworkers who've done the best work
- Anthropic already sets the screening range for very high technical ability
	- next biggest bottleneck for high impact is person's ability to get leverage
		- **leverage** = find and execute work that has a big impact-per-hour multiplier
	- e.g. Anthropic tasks with high impact-per-hour
		- **improving tooling, documentation, dev loops**
			- death by thousand paper cuts - can save so much time in efficient workflows
		- **identifying promising research directions**
		- **system design**
			- tends to be small part of overall time to execute project but can save massive time later
		- **collecting + digging into data**
			- even rote versions of this work well
			- at Anthropic, the most senior researchers and managers do this most often and find the biggest issues otherwise wouldn't have been found
	- many of these start as fraction of a single person's work, then get applied to some problem + work very well
		- and end up becoming major directions
- high-leverage work has 2 interrelated components
	- **agency** = combination of initiative/proactiveness to try make things happen, and relentlessness + resourcefulness to succeed
	- **taste** = good intuition for what things will + won't work well to try
		- important in both picking problems + picking approaches to solve those problems that will work well
	- usually people become great at agency first, then develop taste
		- without taste - you will likely work on the wrong thing
		- without agency, even if you work on the right thing, you likely will go nowhere
	- how to develop these? practicing - no substitute
		- good feedback loops with reality

# 1 Agency
## 1.1 Understand and work backwards from the root goal
- best way to get more leverage -> work on your current goal, or find better way to accomplish the same
	- to do this, need to keep careful track of high level context + priorities your project is aimed at
	- so you can understand and judge the tradeoffs
## 1.2 Don't rely too much on permission/encouragement
- the highest leverage projects don't usually seem like it in advance
	- hence people can be skeptical of pursuing them, usually for the reasons:
		- you cant predict what specific things you might learn or what impact it would have
		- project might seem too hard to execute in a high impact way
- tactic to address this = instead of asking for approval to do something, just tell them what you intend to do
	- implicitly gives them space to course correct if they feel strongly
- another caution is since projects don't always payoff, don't put all your eggs in 1 basket
	- i.e. think of them as bets that might or might not pay off
	- best to keep a portfolio of low-risk projects as well to avoid situation you spend all the time on single high risk project that doesn't pan out
		- and people lose trust to let you pick your own projects again
## 1.3 Make success inevitable
- high-agency people take accountability for achieving a goal, not just doing the work
	- huge difference between these 2 modes
	- **doing the work** = "*My goal is to ship this by end of month, so I'm going to get people started working on it ASAP*"
	- **achieving the goal** = "*My goal is to ship this by end of month, so I'm going to list out everything that needs to be done by then, draw up a schedule working backwards from that date, make sure critical path is short enough, make sure we have enough staffing to do anything, figure out what we'll cut if the schedule slips, be honest about how much slop we need, track progress against the schedule and surface any slippage as soon as I see it, pull in people from elsewhere if I need it*"
- people who get trust for making something inevitable are really rare
	- become a producer of that resource by making your own projects inevitable

# 2 Taste
## 2.1 Find your angle
- taste is often quite unique to people i.e. not much overlap between best taste for something
	- taste is highly idiosyncratic and area-specific (often areas very localised)
	- for this reason -> important signal to keep track of = where is your taste the best?
- how to best find your area of taste = what does it seem like everyone else is mysteriously bad at?
	- probably a good sign you have taste here
## 2.2 Think real hard
- analogy to thinking about your taste = "the quality of your predictive models & search heuristics"
	- *if i design the experiment this way, what will i find?*
	- *if i design the tool this way, how easy will it be to use?*
	- *if i write the doc this way, how much will it resonate with the reader?*
- doing enough search + prediction to come up with great ideas takes time
	- but once you do, it becomes possible to improve your designs by thinking harder about them
	- how to improve this? try unrolling the trajectory of picking a certain action for the project?
		- what do you think will happen?
- things that benefit from more thinking time:
	- **what to work on** -> changing priorities is single biggest lever to improve leverage
	- **design broadly construed** -> e.g. experiment design, system design, org design, process design, blog post design etc
	- **how projects could have gone better + the learnings**
## 2.3 Reflect on your thinking
- the most effective people do the most **metacognition**
	- i.e. reflecting on their own + their team's work + thought processes
	- and figuring out how to improve them
