# taste in the age of infinite information

<div align="center">
  <img src="https://pbs.twimg.com/profile_images/2061456169491951616/MktnuLGE.jpg" width="220" />
</div>

- two distinct types of cognitive labor. on one side, you have the abstract thinkers. the scientists and researchers who write papers and formulate hypotheses. on the other side, you have the builders. the engineers who debug distributed systems and optimize databases. but when you spend enough time observing the absolute best people in both of these domains, this distinction falls apart
- they are both exercises in navigating an almost infinitely vast search space, guided by a deeply trained internal loss function that we colloquially call taste.
- intelligence is essentially just raw compute power. intelligence helps you evaluate a specific path once you have already decided to walk down it. if you have a well defined problem, intelligence will help you crunch the numbers and arrive at the solution faster. but intelligence does not tell you which path to step on in the first place.
- a senior engineer walks in, looks at a single graph of tail latency for a few seconds, and says the serialization library is forcing everything onto a single thread during cryptographic handshakes.
- to an outsider, this looks like magic. it looks like an unexplainable gut feeling. but it is the result of a highly optimized, heavily compressed mental model. over years of breaking and fixing systems, their brain has implicitly trained a neural network to discard the vast majority of the search space instantly. they are not necessarily smarter in terms of raw processing power. they just have a profoundly better heuristic for where the leverage lives.
- human understanding only became possible when physicists stopped looking at individual molecules and invented macro level concepts like pressure, temperature, and entropy. these concepts do not exist at the molecular level. an individual atom does not have a temperature. temperature is a beautiful, deeply compressed abstraction that captures the aggregate structure of all that underlying chaos.
- the history of science tells us that the breakthrough is almost never the solution itself. the breakthrough is discovering the abstraction that makes the solution visible
- but when information is infinite, the only scarce resource left in the entire system is human attention.
- the real outliers will be the ones who can look at a sprawling sea of synthetic possibilities and naturally notice where reality is trying to tell them something important. they will win because they have the taste to know exactly where to dig.
