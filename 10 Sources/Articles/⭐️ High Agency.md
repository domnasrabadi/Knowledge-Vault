# 1 Introduction & Importance

**What is Agency?**  
Agency is the capacity to act, initiate, and influence our environment rather than passively letting life unfold. It's about deciding, "I will make this happen," and then taking steps to see it through. Instead of being purely reactive, people with high agency engage proactively: they view the world not as fixed but as malleable through their actions.

**Why Does It Matter?**  
High agency offers clear benefits:
- **Greater Problem-Solving:** More agency means you're more likely to tackle big challenges and find creative solutions.
- **Enhanced Resilience:** High-agency people adapt quickly because they see setbacks as obstacles to overcome rather than final verdicts.
- **Innovation & Impact:** Whether in personal projects or at work, a proactive approach often leads to breakthroughs and leadership opportunities.

**A Core Mindset: "There Is Only Now"**
- Life is a series of finite "nows."
- The more we act in the moment, the more we shape the future.

# 2 Key Components of Agency

High agency centres on three elements (like the three wheels of a tricycle): Clear Thinking, Bias to Action, and Disagreeability. Remove one, and you lose the full force of agency.

1. **Clear Thinking**
    - Ability to reason independently, avoid mental traps, and refine problems into simpler terms.
    - Involves questioning assumptions rather than passively accepting them.
2. **Bias to Action**
    - Preference for doing rather than endlessly planning or waiting for perfect conditions.
    - Even small steps can break inertia and build momentum.
3. **Disagreeability**
    - Willingness to push against norms or collective opinion when necessary.
    - Not about being hostile; it's about having the backbone to stick to convictions despite external pressure.

**Symptoms of High-Agency Individuals**
- **Energising Presence:** After interacting with them, you often feel motivated or inspired.
- **Unpredictable Opinions:** They don't simply echo common sentiment; they form their own conclusions.
- **Verifying Instead of Trusting:** "Who is 'they'?" "Show me the primary source."
- **Weird Teenage Hobbies & Niche Interests:** They explore offbeat subjects, often spotting trends before the crowd.
- **Mean to Your Face, Nice Behind Your Back:** They offer honest critiques in person but speak well of you to others.
- **Immigrant Mentality:** They act like they have everything to gain through resourcefulness and grit.
- **Who Would You Call in a Crisis?**
    - High-agency folks are those you'd trust to help if you were stuck in a 3rd-world jail cell. They thrive under pressure, improvising solutions on the fly.

# 3 The Stages of Agency Development

Agency evolves over time. Recognising your current stage can help you progress deliberately:

1. **Goal-Directed (Reactive)**
    - Meets immediate demands and deadlines.
    - Good at routine tasks but doesn't regularly set proactive goals.
2. **Intentional**
    - Strategically plans actions in advance.
    - Begins to focus on medium or longer-term objectives.
3. **Rational (Strategic Thinking)**
    - Uses metacognition to refine and adjust problem-solving approaches.
    - Capable of analyzing choices from multiple angles.
4. **Social-Normative**
    - Understands and navigates team or community norms effectively.
    - Skilled at collaboration and managing a professional reputation.
5. **Self-Authoring**
    - Develops a personal vision beyond the group consensus.
    - Sets and pursues goals aligned with core values and identity.
6. **Self-Transforming**
    - Continuously revisits and reshapes assumptions.
    - Embraces adaptive thinking to innovate over the long haul.

# 4 Overcoming Low-Agency Traps

Everyone encounters mental or emotional hurdles. Here's how to spot and escape them:

1. **Vague Trap**
    - **Symptom:** Indecisiveness from fuzzy goals or undefined problems.
    - **Escape Route:** Transform thoughts onto paper or another medium. Keep refining until the problem is clearly stated.
2. **Midwit Trap**
    - **Symptom:** Overcomplicating everything; equating simplicity with ignorance.
    - **Escape Route:** Ask, "What would the simplest approach be?" or "How would I ensure I fail?" then invert those answers to find straightforward solutions.
3. **Attachment Trap**
    - **Symptom:** Being stuck in old assumptions; treating them like unchangeable facts.
    - **Escape Route:** Ask, "What if I had 10x the agency?" This reframes your mindset to seek fresh perspectives.
4. **Rumination Trap**
    - **Symptom:** Paralysis by "what if it goes wrong?" loops.
    - **Escape Route:** Frame decisions as experiments, reducing the fear of failure. Action ends unproductive overthinking.
5. **Overwhelm Trap**
    - **Symptom:** Feeling buried by the sheer scale of a task.
    - **Escape Route:** Identify the smallest logical step. Start there, building momentum.

# 5 Building & Applying High Agency

**Practical Ways to Grow Your Agency**
1. **Ask for Things (Even Unreasonable)**
    - If you're never hearing "no," you're not aiming high enough. "Court rejection" to expand your comfort zone.
2. **Seek Feedback Intensely**
    - Make it safe and easy for others to give honest input. Use it to iterate quickly.
3. **Embrace an "Everything Is Learnable" Attitude**
    - Treat new challenges as skills you haven't acquired yet. Dive in with the assumption it's possible to learn.
4. **"Does This Defy the Laws of Physics?"**
    - If the answer is no, it's solvable in principle—activate your creativity to find a way.

**There Is No "Normal"**
- Hiding quirks or conforming too tightly stifles agency.
- Memorable and meaningful actions often break routine expectations (e.g., traveling across the world for a friend's birthday, picking up the tab for everyone, or giving brutally honest feedback).

**Imperfect "Greats"**  
Even history's icons struggled with flaws or misguided pursuits:
- Steve Jobs delayed cancer treatment to try a carrot juice diet and acupuncture.
- Mozart died in debt and often begged friends for money.
- Nietzsche died bitter and alone, having sold only a few hundred copies of his work.
- Henry Ford ran an anti-Semitic newspaper campaign.

**Key Takeaway:** You don't have to be superhuman. Everyone is deeply human, and greatness emerges from persistent agency despite imperfections.

**High Agency at Work**
- **Continuous Learning:** Keep retooling your mind, skills, and frameworks.
- **Leadership Development:** High agency helps you lead teams and drive change.
- **Organisational Impact:** Proactive individuals elevate group performance and foster a culture of innovation.

# 6 Conclusion & Next Steps

High agency is about shaping your life path, refusing to be a passenger. By staying focused on clear thinking, a bias to action, and having the courage to differ, you strengthen your capacity to handle complex challenges and leave a meaningful mark.

**7-Step Exercise**
1. **Open a Blank Page:** Write down an abstract value you admire (e.g., _High Agency_).
2. **Generate 10 Specific Actions:** Brain-dump possible ways to _manifest_ this value in real life—don't overthink.
3. **Pick One, Especially If It Scares You:** The one that triggers excitement or fear is your best bet.
4. **Break It Down:** List micro-steps (e.g., find someone's email address, draft a letter, hit send).
5. **Act Immediately:** Do each micro-step now.
6. **Notice the Feeling:** Embrace the rush of being a "live player." You're making ideas real.
7. **Repeat Tomorrow:** Over time, these daily leaps compound into major transformations.

**Final Thought:**
> "If it doesn't defy the laws of physics, there's a way." — Keep challenging the boundaries. Develop the conviction that reality is negotiable through persistent, creative action.
