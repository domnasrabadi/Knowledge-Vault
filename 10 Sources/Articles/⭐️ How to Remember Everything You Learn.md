# Understanding & Encoding
- **preview first (survey the structure)**
	- always begin w quick overview → skim TOC, headings, intro, summary 
	- big-picture-first approach gives your brain a framework to slot details into 
- **ask questions & set intentions**
    - turn headings into questions → read specifically to find answers
    - focuses attention & transforms passive intake into active hunt
- **layered reading (multiple passes)**
    - read in layers of depth (skim → analytical → deep dive)
    - prevents getting bogged down in details before understanding context
- **active > passive reading**
    - mark up text, highlight sparingly, paraphrase in own words
    - forces brain to process/rephrase ideas vs. just moving eyes over text
- **the 20% highlighting rule**
    - limit highlighting to 10–20% of text → mark only the essence
    - forces evaluation/prioritization vs. mental laziness of over-highlighting
- **note density & paraphrasing**
    - keep notes brief & use personal vocabulary
    - if notes are as long as text, you aren't summarizing; paraphrasing improves recall
- **focused attention (eliminate distractions)**
    - silence phone/close tabs to ensure new info registers in memory
    - ritualize deep-focus sessions (e.g., 30-min blocks) to enter "flow" state
- **understand before judging**
    - delay critical judgment until you can clearly summarize author's position
    - prevents emotional distraction/bias from blocking full comprehension
- **pacing and chunking**
    - break reading into segments w short breaks to recap
    - working memory limit (4–7 items) requires "batching" to avoid blur
- **time budgeting (80/20 rule)**
    - spend 80% of time on the 20% of content that yields most insight

# Memory & Retention
- **spaced repetition (spacing effect)**
    - review info at strategically increasing intervals (1 day, 1 week, 1 month)
    - builds resistance to forgetting by reinforcing memory just as it starts to fade
- **active recall (retrieval practice)**
    - test yourself without looking at material (e.g., "Blank Sheet" approach)
    - effort of retrieval strengthens neural pathways ~50% more than re-reading
- **mnemonic devices & association**
    - use acronyms or Memory Palace (Method of Loci) for complex info
    - leverages visual-spatial memory by tying abstract ideas to vivid mental images
- **chunking information**
    - group related bits into larger "chunks" (e.g., nesting facts under themes)
    - reduces cognitive load by bypassing short-term memory limit ($7 \pm 2$)
- **interleaving practice**
    - mix different subjects/topics in one session instead of "block" studying
    - creates "desirable difficulty" that prevents autopilot & improves differentiation
- **elaboration & dual coding**
    - connect new info to old knowledge ("why/how") & use visuals/diagrams
    - creates multiple recall pathways (verbal + visual) for the same idea
- **immediate review & 24-hour rule**
    - revisit material within 24 hours to stop 70–80% of initial forgetting
    - signals brain that info is worth consolidating during sleep
- **"one month later" test**
    - true learning is measured by what is retained after weeks, not minutes
    - set reminders to check for memory gaps & schedule refreshers

# Synthesis & Integration
- **make connections (latticework of mental models)**
    - link new facts to established mental frameworks across disciplines
    - creates a web of knowledge where each node reinforces the others
- **feynman technique (explain it simply)**
    - explain concept in plain terms as if teaching a 12-year-old
    - pinpointing where you struggle to simplify reveals gaps in understanding
- **zettelkasten & atomic notes**
    - break ideas into single "atomic" notes linked via references
    - external system mirrors a connected brain & generates new insights
- **progressive summarization**
    - distill info in layers (highlight → bold → one-page summary)
    - repeated processing at decreasing lengths ensures depth without oversimplifying
- **balance breadth vs. depth**
    - skim many sources to filter fluff; invest deep re-reading in top 1%
    - re-reading high-density material yields more than 10 mediocre first-reads
- **when to re-read vs. move on**
    - re-read foundational/rich material; move on from poor writing/filler
    - re-reading is a strategic tool for mastery, not a compulsive habit
- **apply and synthesize as you go**
    - convert reading into usable output (essays, decisions, teaching)
    - moves info from passive recognition to active, integrated skill
