## Highlights
- measurement = process of obtaining information about a system’s capabilities, impacts, opportunities, and risks
    - can be qualitative or quantitative
    - forms the evidence that evaluators rely on
- measurement framework = structure grounded in social-science measurement theory for evaluating generative ai systems
    - aims to make measurements reliable and meaningful
- four-level hierarchy = scaffold that connects abstract ideas to concrete data
    - background concept = broad constellation of possible meanings tied to an idea
    - systematized concept = explicit formulation selected from that constellation
    - measurement instrument = procedures or artifacts (classifiers, annotation guidelines, scoring rules) that capture data
    - instance-level measurement = the specific labels, scores, or other outputs produced by an instrument
- linking processes
    - systematization = moving from background concept to systematized concept through debate and definition
    - operationalization = turning the systematized concept into a workable measurement instrument
    - application = using the instrument to create instance-level measurements
- evaluation of ml systems = making judgements about whether to use, deploy, or redesign a model based on measurement evidence
- generative ai measurement challenges
    - concepts such as creativity, bias, or harm are complex, nuanced, and often contested
    - no universal ground truth, so validating measurements is difficult
    - resembles long-standing measurement problems in the social sciences
- benefits of explicit systematization
    - lets diverse stakeholders (open-source developers, policymakers, marginalized communities, users) participate in defining concepts
    - forces transparency about which meanings are included or excluded
    - adds rigor when no agreed-upon labels exist, improving operational debates
