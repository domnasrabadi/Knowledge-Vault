- Big projects often lose out to quick dopamine hits from small information snacks
    - The Zettelkasten method uses short attention spans productively
    - In small free moments, instead of social media or a big reading project, you can do small things with small notes
- Zettelkasten is German for “slip box”
    - In analog form, a box filled with slips of paper containing notes and metadata
    - Organises paper in a non-hierarchical way
- Purpose is not to find a single note, but to explore connections between notes
    - Helps collect knowledge for writing and reveals research gaps
    - Linking and keywording can lead to unexpected insights
- Helps retrieve knowledge when memory fails so it can be turned into writing
    - Also helps decide what to read next and develop ideas
    - Frees mental energy to generate new ideas
- **Underlying purposes**
    - Retain what you read — repeated exposure to the most interesting parts of what you’ve read
    - Retrieve it quickly — find and copy exact quotes in seconds
    - Know what to read next — follow curiosity productively
    - Develop ideas — start with written passages for an instant first draft
    - Have ideas — free mental bandwidth for thinking
- Writing down what you read improves retention
    - Re-writing in your own words is more powerful than copying word-for-word (except for quotes)
    - Only capture important, interesting, or relevant things
- Re-writing, keywording, and linking notes prompts associative thinking, which can improve mood
- Search engines are not a substitute for your own notes
    - Managing notes solidifies memory and stores ideas in progress
- Tools are like bicycle gears — different ones suit different conditions
    - High-power tools can bring distraction; choose based on focus depth needed
- **Digital notes advantages**
    - Searchability
    - Portability
    - Backup
    - Speed
    - Mutability
    - Dynamism
    - Collaboration
- **Paper notes advantages**
    - Tactility
    - Focus
    - Retention
    - Privacy
- Plain-text files are recommended for digital Zettelkasten
    - Data-portable
    - Simple
    - Can use Markdown for formatting without leaving keyboard
    - Lightweight
    - Fast to search and load
    - Offline-compatible
    - Future-proof
- Markdown enables formatting (headers, bold, italics) without vendor lock-in
    - Readable as raw text or rendered view
    - **WikiLinks/Freelink** lets you link between Markdown files in plain text
- **Three main note types**
    - Fleeting notes — captured on the fly
    - Literature notes — condensed summaries of a work
    - Permanent notes — single ideas with metadata and links
- **Additional folders**
    - Inbox — unprocessed fleeting notes or highlights
    - Someday/Maybe — interesting but not currently relevant items
    - Raw — unprocessed exported highlights from sources
- Fleeting notes
    - Casual, possibly disorganised
    - Just enough info to decide later whether to process further
- Literature notes
    - Informal summaries of consumed media
    - In your own words (except for direct quotes)
    - Writing them improves understanding
- Permanent notes
    - Single ideas annotated with subject, related notes, and source
    - Derived from literature notes
    - Core building blocks of your Zettelkasten and final stop before synthesis into new work
- Re-writing and condensing ensures only the clearest and most compelling elements survive
    - Internalises knowledge further
- Only write notes you need to learn or retrieve
    - Threshold decisions: digital storage is cheap, but time and mental energy are not
    - Balance between writing a lot and preserving energy for your own ideas
- Information density and language complexity vary by medium
- **Absorption process**
    - Make fleeting notes while reading (highlights, margin notes, separate notebook)
    - Export highlights to Markdown (Readwise can automate)
    - For paper books, either highlight highlights or condense them
    - Digital reading allows searchability, portability, and access to unhighlighted text
- Highlight what’s relevant to your work
    - Progressive summarisation — highlight the highlights
    - Re-write the best ones in your own words to make literature notes
- Turning literature notes into permanent notes
    - Extract the most interesting ideas and make one permanent note per idea
- Not all materials get processed through all steps — depends on interest and relevance to current projects
- Choosing the right keywords or tags makes the Zettelkasten a non-hierarchical knowledge database
- **Linking vs tagging**
    - Tags group related notes via search
    - Links create more prominent direct connections
    - Both can be useful
- Typical workflow
    - Use computer time for linking, tagging, and organising
    - Summarise fleeting notes while reclining
    - Read lying down
    - Take fleeting notes while active
- **Turning notes into completed work**
    - Goal of Zettelkasten is to produce writing
    - Note-taking solidifies knowledge and creates miniature drafts
- **Tag indexes**
    - Review all notes with a tag quickly
    - Tag index gives additional context and can form an outline
    - Rearrange into draft order, then re-write in a new document
- Experiment with metadata levels — note what info you repeatedly fetch
