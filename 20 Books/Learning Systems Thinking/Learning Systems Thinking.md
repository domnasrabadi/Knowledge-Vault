# 1 What is Systems Thinking?
- complexity of everything has increased 
	- e.g. the internet now grown into a super complex information graph no one ever designed it to be
	- lots of issues with keeping up with all this info 
		- main reason = cannot change our thinking fast enough to keep up 
	- technology design == communication design 
		- constrained to produce designs which are copies of the communication structures we use 
		- communication = how we think together, structure + flow of info between our minds, others, etc 
- our thinking generates the concepts that we rely on when designing our systems 
	- what we think + communicate = what we push to production 
	- without conceptual integrity = systems are many good but independent, uncoordinated ideas 
		- wilderness of systems, there is a lot less ***concrete***
- linear thinking is the default we learn/use throughout life 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">linear thinking</mark> = **reductionistic, understand whole by breaking it into parts e.g. OOP** 
		- if this, then that, casual thinking - software behaves as we intend it to 
		- code is unambiguous 
	- when we outgrow the model, we recreate the boxes 
	- linear thinking has it's benefits 
		- breaks complex problem into component parts 
		- analyses cause and effect 
		- helps imagine the steps involved in building then executing them 
		- identifies weak areas + plans improvements 
		- identify + follow best practices
		- test ideas + discover failures 
		- improve efficiency by editing solutions till they are elegantly simple 
- however as relational complexity increases, need to think differently + systematically 
	- systems thinking is nonlinear 

> linear systems have an important modular virtue: you can take them apart and put them together again, they add up. 

> nonlinear systems can't do this, this twisted changeability makes it hard to calculate but also creates rich kinds of behaviour that never occur in linear systems 

- most systems in real world are nonlinear
	- results are often caused by a combination of factors - not one main one
	- nonlinear thinking is hard to do + requires practice
		- but it improves you capacity for doing difficult things
		- includes learning all the time + thinking deeply + thinking deeply about thinking 
	- many types/combinations of systems thinking
		- nonlinear thinking, pattern thinking, parallel thinking, systemic reasoning 
		- all of which think in systems 
- what is a system? what is systems + thinking?
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">system</mark> = group of interrelated hardware, software, people, organisations and other elements that interact and/or interdepend to serve a shared purpose 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">systems thinking</mark> = the practice of recognises + understanding the links, relationships, interactions among the components of a system that give rise to the system's observed behaviour 
- **qualities of a systems thinker**
	1. *practice thinking*
	2. *recognise difference between linear + nonlinear, when to apply them* 
	3. *knows solutions meaningfully connected to the context* 
	4. *knows people systems are inextricable from technical systems* 
	5. *shifts perspective easily to explore challenges* 
	6. *demonstrates high levels of self awareness*
	7. *avoids adding noise and blame, responding not reacting* 
	8. *approach life w "always learning" mindset* 
	9. *communicates well-reasoned ideas, recommendations + theories* 
	10. *listens respectfully* 
	11. *understand how interrelated + interdependent parts act together + create patterns, processes*
	12. *creates conceptual models* 
	13. *accepts that uncertainty is natural* 
- 2 reasons why systems thinking is hard 
	- takes practice to skilfully + consistently untangle ourselves from linear thinking
	- many biases + unknowns when trying to think nonlinearly 
# 2 Crafting Conceptual Integrity 
- what is conceptual integrity?
	- everything running on production = our concepts, ideas prioritised, communicated, structured and adapted w others, which are then crafted into code 
		- concepts become actionable when people in an org communicate them 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">conceptual integrity</mark> = ideas are cohesive, in good relationship, supported by healthy shared patterns and principles + pushing code changes improves the system's ability to serve it's purpose 
		- involves understanding the whole, not just the parts we optimise 
- signs of lacking conceptual integrity 
	- product teams avoid building "the right way" because its too hard 
	- no shared semantically meaningful data structure among software 
	- difficult to tell what the parts are there to do
	- tech debt is downstream result 
- **relationships produce effect** 
	- parts together produce an effect different from the effect of each part on its own 
		- involves understanding the effect produced by relationships 
	- consider these key questions for thinking about a system 
		1. *how do parts interdepend?* 
		2. *how do they share information?* 
		3. *what are the patterns that keep the relationships functioning?* 
		4. *what are the patterns that block the system from evolving?* 
		5. *can we improve the relationships/patterns to deliver the highest value outcomes?* 
	- relationships form circumstances 
		- those circumstances depend on patterns 
		- when events change those patterns in unpredictable ways, parts of the system are impacted in unpredictable ways 
- systems thinking is socio-technical (people + tech)
	- thinking, behaviours + communication patterns are inextricable from the software we produce 

> you cannot improve a technology system without improving the people system, vice versa 
> they are one and the same from a systems perspective

- our thinking is influenced by code we've already delivered!
	- team = collection of people, thinking systems = relationships among team members 
- systems design can be done top down or bottom up (both have tradeoffs)
	- systems design is in between top down + bottom up 
	- empowered teams have ability to self organise collaborative work 
		- sharing knowledge to enable positive impacts on system as a whole 
- counter-intuitiveness 
	- when we want to change a system, we should always look for leverage points
		- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">leverage points</mark> = places in a system where a small shift can produce big changes in everything 
	- we always have blind spots - e.g. adding more people to a late project, makes the project even later 
		- systems thinking is about proactively looking for these
	- actions taken in a system are *always* an experiment
		- can never be certain we get the right result
		- but increase our chances with reasoning that the action is sound, relevant and cohesive 
	- because we need to think counterintuitively - sometimes we will be alone
		- no validation or support for the idea
		- and we might even change our mind since we think our thinking is unsound 
- systems perspective depends on understanding what has changed
	- we learn through experience (both good + recurring frustrating stuff)
	- we evolve our concepts as we learn 
	- changes in a system, regardless of the level of relational complexity, happen over time
- time as a change factor 
	- systems are always changed by time - because the state changes over time 
	- feedback time can be short or long - longer feedback loops are more challenging
		- but hence we need insight through observability + monitoring 
	- questions to consider 
		1. *how do the circumstances change over time?* 
		2. *what happens in response to a high impact event?* 
		3. *what is the root cause of that event?* 
		4. *why do things happen how they do + when they happen?*
- sit at the front of the train
	- each passenger might see what's out their own window, but you need to see it all in front 
		- e.g. the forest for the trees
	- there is no technology/group/solution etc that will work universally 
	- what works in any circumstance *depends*
		- depends on the context, people, communication flow etc......
	- how to be right more often 
		- see things as they are + can be 
		- work w others to get input to strengthen your thinking
		- gracefully let go of wrong views 
		- be patient, kind, curious about others perspectives while you learn 
- **systems thinking is figuring out what “it depends” on**
	- then communicating new ways of seeing until people can see it
	- to become good at communicating, you’ll need to practice thinking and communicating your thinking
- writing is a good way to practice thinking
	- consider writing this when thinking about a systems change
		1. *what was the goal? what was the process for changing or improving something?*
		2. *how were ideas being shared? what structure or template was used? where was it stored?*
		3. *whose ideas were they? what ideas were proposed that could've also worked?*
		4. *what was the output? how did it match the goal? how do you know?* 
		5. *what was the impact of time? longer term impacts? downstream impacts?*
		6. *did counter-intuitiveness play a role?* 
		7. *do you see any opportunities to improve the process you just described?*
# 3 Shifting your Perspective 
- to create conceptual integrity 
	- your perspective = a concept, general notion of what is happening
	- for integrity, need to look at circumstances through multiple perspectives + see it more broadly 
- thinking w others
	- can shine a light on you concepts you have not seen yet 
	- when we think together, we synthesise perspectives to create more comprehensive perspectives 
	- importantly, need to also use a shared vocabulary 
		- words are powerful containers to encapsulate a common POV 
- 5 core practices 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">you are constantly learning</span> 
		- knowing there is always more to know, you are a proactive learner 
		- you adopt new tools/approaches then they are helpful + relevant 
		- don't mind being a beginner + learning from others' experiences 
		- you discern what to learn
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">you're comfortable structuring inquiry</span> 
		- you figure out how to figure something out, create a strategy for understanding it better 
		- you look up articles + papers, speak to people w different perspectives
		- you build a prototype to test ideas
		- you make a list of questions to answer then add questions that help understand from different perspectives
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">you do deep work</span> 
		- you invest in distraction free thinking time
			- becoming aware of structuring, strengthening + improving your thinking
			- concentration is key 
		- however you practice (daily, weekly, regularly), you get focused generative work done 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">you respectfully engage</span> 
		- when others share their thinking, you acknowledge what the person has said + consider it 
		- find the good, then if possible help strengthen their reasoning to make the idea stronger 
		- when doing this - notice how often your first reaction is "no"
			- the belittler isn't often correct, even when they are, they just make communication harder
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">you experiment + learn from mistakes </span>
		- you play a glue role, holding parts, people, teams together 
		- you facilitate discussion to help overcome resistance to thinking together
			- ask questions like:
				- *"what convinced you?"*
				- *"why does it matter that we do that?"*
		- if you experiment, you can be correct or learn from shifts in perspectives 
- modelling is a core practice 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">modelling</mark> involves visualising the concept + relationships between them 
	- modelling is an action verb 
		- **process of thinking about a system visually** 
		- you model like you write - explore ideas, gain insight, communicate your ideas, synthesise knowledge to understand how a system works
	- modelling = method of inquiry, to learn, think, understand + see things from multiple POVs
		- **modelling makes our thinking + understanding visible + explicit** 
	- use modelling whenever you struggle to find common ground or get stuck 
		- modelling before coding also supports our confidence 
- example of questions to explore through modelling 

| **System concept**               | **What you might model**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What is the current state?**   | What is happening now? <br>How does information flow in and how does it flow out currently? <br>What is happening when the information is at rest and in motion? <br>What is its structure and how does the structure change? <br>What data is available about the timing; what does a chart of response times look like? |
| **What is the goal?**            | Define “too slow.” <br>How fast should the information be received? <br>Why is “faster” valuable—will it enable the system to serve its purpose? <br>How do we know that changing this discrepancy is “better”? <br>Better for whom and in what ways? <br>Are there other goals that supersede this one?                  |
| **What is the discrepancy?**     | What is the time difference between the current time and goal time? <br>Under what circumstances does “slow” occur? <br>How do we measure it? <br>Do our measurements actually measure the problem we are solving? <br>What happens during the delay and how is it being reinforced? <br>What is impacted by the delay?   |
| **What is the perceived state?** | We say “too long,” but what benefits do we get from the delay? <br>What are the tradeoffs involved in making it faster? <br>Does everyone perceive this as a “problem”? If not, why not? <br>If making it faster has impact elsewhere, who will perceive those changes as harmful or helpful?                             |

## 3.1 Iceberg Model
- we fix, adapt, patch, plan better features
	- whenever an event re-occurs, we take action to prevent it w wider test coverage
		- but problem is that systems will do this endlessly 
	- as long as we stay at surface level, we are likely to reinforce whatever is causing the problem 
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">iceberg model</mark> = guides our thinking into the underlying patterns, system structures + mental models involved in an event 
	- root causes are almost always our mental models 

![[Screenshot 2025-03-21 at 3.22.04 pm.webp| center | 500]]

- layers to the iceberg model 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">events</mark> 
		- what happened? 
		- what was visible? 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">patterns and trends</mark> 
		- has this happened before? 
		- when? under what circumstances?
		- has resistance blocked other problems from being addressed?
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">structure</mark> 
		- what org/social structures, rules, rituals etc support the pattern(s)?
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">mental models</mark>
		- what do we believe/value that gives rise to those structures and patterns?
- the iceberg model reveals potential leverage points 
	- places to change in the system that give lasting impact 
- shifting perspective is hard 
	- aren't simply tweaking our thinking with these practices ^
	- we are detangling our thoughts, experiences, mental models, feelings, communication patterns 
		- detangling is deep work 
	- need to consider alternative approaches + perspectives
		- even when it would be easier to think + act linearly 
# 4 Self-Awareness as a foundational skill 
- systems thinking is learning all the time 
	- structuring your inquiry in ways that generate relevant knowledge 
- impossible to improve your thinking if you aren't aware of your thinking
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">self awareness</mark> == meta-cognition == **observe + adapt your own thoughts**
	- means knowing your weaknesses + knowing your strengths and what motivates you 
- systems thinking is grounded in metacognition - critical awareness of your own thought processes 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">critical thinking</mark> = ability to analyse + evaluate situations
	- your critical thinking skills depend on:
		- quality + cohesiveness of your own thinking process
		- how you objectively interpret thoughts, feelings + experiences which lead you to chosen actions 
		- your ability to reach sound conclusions via a path of observation, consideration + inquiry 
- your thoughts arise as result of systemic patterns you've experienced 
	- self-awareness = noticing how you react to your experiences + understanding how you learn best + how you change your mind
	- you consume information from external + internal sources
		- your mind interprets these + responds based on many factors
		- your thoughts generate your actions 
		- and vice versa.... reinforcing feedback loops 
	- self awareness helps you discover mental models at the bottom of your own iceberg 
- why systems thinking is hard 
	- developing tolerance for ambiguity/uncertainty that systems require 
		- ambiguity = open to more than one interpretation
	- there is no *One Answer* for me to discover
		- **there is only tradeoffs, letting go of good ideas in favour of one idea that better serves the circumstance**
	- linear thinking processes are designed to construct certainty 
		- but in systems, you cannot be certain... about anything 
		- when we accept this, we leave space for curiosity, learning + observing what happens we set our thoughts in motion 
- decision making is noisy 
	- self awareness helps you uncover your true goals 
		- e.g. doing tricky coding problem your stuck on, might get hungry and go buy some food
		- you might be hungry, you might think food will help your brain to think better 
		- but true goal might be avoiding discomfort + uncertainty of facing the problem 
			- and secondary goal is dopamine rush of sugar/salty food 
	- our minds supply endless flow of information in form of ideas
		- and we constantly consume more ideas + information 
			- some thoughts are triggered by anxiety and shout their way to top of mind 
		- **we need to discern what thoughts to prioritise**
			- i.e. distinguishing signal from noise 
			- signal = idea, info, insight, concept that points your attention in a matterful direction 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">discernment</mark> = **ability to understand situations, make decisions, even when there is no "concrete" right answer**
		- more complex a situation == more discernment you need to navigate it 
		- also involves not just knowing what to think about but **when to think about it**
	- people tend to focus on trivial (noise) matters - easier to solve + at surface of iceberg 
		- rather than focus on more complex matters - patterns, structures, mental models (signal)
	- modelling helps discernment 
- how to observe your thinking
	- nonlinear approaches always begin w observation 
		- we observe throughout the entire lifecycle of a project 
	- need to train ourselves to pay attention to our thinking
		- one practice is just writing whatever comes to mind 
		- one rule is to just keep writing whatever comes to mind - literally writing your thoughts 
		- other methods = walking, rhythmic movement, meditation, discourse, doing art 
- what self-awareness teaches you 
	- *quality of my delivery == how well I can discern between good thinking vs habituated thinking* 
	- *level of psychological safety in my environment correlated to quality of work I can deliver* 
	- *fighting others' thinking usually waste of energy, demonstrating sound thinking gets you what you need* 
	- *my thoughts are not always truthful, reasonable or intelligent* 
	- *quality of my thinking improves when I'm focused, degrades when I get busy* 
	- *I am prone to being swayed by others' opinions*
	- *I make bad decisions when emotions either lead the way, or are ignored entirely* 
# 5 Replace Reacting w Responding 
- everything you do is a result of stimuli 
	- results in you experiences, thoughts, emotions, physical sensations, attitudes, impulses etc
	- might not realise how often your thinking is controlled by your reactions

![[Screenshot 2025-03-30 at 9.55.11 am.webp| center | 500]]

- ideally - you will *experience* a reaction, and *contribute* a response
- the most challenging reactive patterns = those that happen over and over again
	- death by a thousand paper cuts 
		- reactions are highly combustible - we react to a reaction of a reaction of a ...
		- reactions are usually defensive - making them unreliable 
	- self awareness = allows us to notice the heat generated by our reactions
		- and practice experiencing them without setting the situation on fire
- opinions are a form of reaction - with downsides
	- we debate dualities - but nuanced intersectional thinking is uncommon + unwelcome
	- we want to be right - we love our opinions and defend them - antithetical to systems thinking
	- we ignore context - opinions don't usually focus on understanding complexity inherent in each context
	- we are emotional, we focus on "wrong" 
- empathy is a key skills 
	- we should reach conclusions by identifying w others POVs
		- knowing we are closely linked to that POV since we are from same system 
	- empathising != condoning, don't have to agree with it
- understanding root cause takes time + consideration 
	- need to give ourselves that time 
	- 4 ways to practice working w reactions
		- notice them without judging them 
		- look for systemic information in our reactions 
		- don't suppress them 
		- manage your own reactions, let others do the same 
- options when reacting
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">"Yes, and ..."</span>
		- when someone raises an issue, acknowledge it (don't have to agree)
		- example
			- problem = *"need the API response times to be faster because too many client connections are dropping after three seconds"*
			- reaction = *"response times are fast enough. That’s not the problem*"
			- yes-and = *"Thanks, Hollis, for describing the impact of response times. I wasn’t aware of slow response experiences. What alerted you to this problem?"*
		- = a respectful approach to discussing ideas
			- **acknowledge what the person has said and adding to it** 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">24 Hour rule</span>
		- wait 24 hours to respond 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">Breathe</span>
		- box breathing or 5 minutes of breathing practice
		- notice your thoughts before and after 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">Go for a walk </span>
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">Make a snack or take a nap</span>
		- HALT = hungry, angry, lonely, tired
			- tend to overreact when these qualities are present 
		- if you must act under HALT, try do the smallest thing possible
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">Write</span>
		- set a timer, write out everything happening in your mind by hand
- when we don’t understand what’s going on, blame tries to fill the gaps
	- blame rarely helps us solve the problem 
# 6 A System of Learning 
- goal of systems thinking = focus on generative learning
	- increasing our capacity for synthesising knowledge and experience 
- learning goals for a systems thinker
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Improve your ability to shift perspective</mark> 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Increase your tolerance for ambiguity</mark> 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Understand context and relational impact</mark> 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Identify patterns and structures Create groupings and boundaries without reductionism</mark> 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Think critically and apply sound judgment</mark> 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Develop effective interpersonal skills</mark>
- data vs information vs knowledge vs wisdom
	- learning = continuum of increasing contextual/relational complexity 
		- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">data</span> = raw materials, facts
		- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">information</span> = conceptual, there is nuance in information 
			- **nuance increases as we move towards knowledge + wisdom**
		- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">knowledge</span> = fluid mix of framed experience, values, contextual information, expert insight and grounded intuition that provides an environment and framework for evaluating and incorporating new experiences and information
			- knowledge relies on justifiable inference 
			- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">inference</span> = reaching a conclusion based on reasoning + evidence 
		- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">wisdom</span> = applying knowledge in ways that generate growth in a meaningful, impactful direction 
	- understanding is knowing why the knowledge matters 
		- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">understanding</span> = **discerning how knowledge enables growth in a particular context**

![[Screenshot 2025-03-30 at 10.31.12 am.webp| center | 500]]

- systems thinking definitions for similar terms 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">knowledge</span> = ability to generate artefacts (models, documents, code, software), routines, processes, practices, and cultural norms that are insightful and valuable in a particular context
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">learning</span> = ability to craft knowledge
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">understanding</span> = ability to discern which knowledge will be most effective in a particular context.
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">wisdom</span> = ability to discover true leverage points in the systems we inhabit and push them in a valuable direction
- knowledge stock vs flow
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">knowledge stock</mark> = store of knowledge you have developed or can access
		- generally increases your *efficiency*
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">knowledge flow</mark> = ability to transfer knowledge between people/tech in ways that change/shift the system
		- generally increases you *effectiveness*

> information is a recipe. 
> knowledge is a cook. 
> wisdom is a chef. 

## 6.1 A Learning Driven Career
- when you put learning at the center of your career - you scale
	- there is no way in to think in systems w/o developing personal mastery
		- = a deep understanding of your learning process + how to improve it 
## 6.2 Four methods to integrate into your learning experience 
- including: generating artefacts, observe + inquire, synthesise knowledge, gain experience
- 1. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">generate artefacts</mark>
	- knowledge work is inextricable from language + structure of communication 
		- we use language, symbols, reasoning to construct our concepts and share them 
	- examples of *artefacts* to increase knowledge ***stock*** and ***flow***

| Job Role           | Knowledge Stock                                                   | Knowledge Flow                                                               |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| AI scientist       | research paper or preprint<br>experimental model code             | peer review workshops<br>collaborative paper discussions                     |
| AI project manager | project roadmap documentations<br>risk/impact analysis            | cross functional team workshops<br>stakeholder briefings + feedback meetings |
| Data scientist     | working papers or Jupyter notebooks<br>custom data visualisations | presentations<br>collaborative code reviews                                  |

- 2. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">observe and inquire</mark> 
	- in systems, the questions you generate are more valuable than the answers you discover 
		- when learning, incorporate observing + letting your curiosity generate questions 
	- examples of *observe + inquire activities* for increasing knowledge ***stock*** and ***flow***

| Job Role              | Knowledge Stock                                    | Knowledge Flow                                              |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| AI Research Scientist | Literature Deep-Dives<br>Experiment Observation    | Research Group Brainstorms<br>Interdisciplinary Workshops   |
| AI Project Manager    | Data-Driven Reviews<br>Process Evaluation          | Retrospective Sessions<br>Stakeholder Dialogues             |
| Data Scientist        | Code and Data Exploration<br>Dataset Investigation | Collaborative Analysis Reviews<br>Interactive Data Sessions |

- 3. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">synthesise knowledge</mark>
	- where familiar learning activities happen e.g. reading, watching talks, podcasts, taking a course, working w others
		- goal is to synthesise other's knowledge + experience with your own 
		- different from accumulating information
			- **aim is to identify and understand the patterns + core concepts that matter**
	- examples of *synthesising* activities to increase knowledge ***stock*** and ***flow***

| Job Role              | Knowledge Stock                                              | Knowledge Flow                                                  |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| AI Research Scientist | Reading AI research books<br>Attending AI conferences        | Publishing research reviews<br>Hosting AI workshops             |
| AI Project Manager    | Reading leadership books<br>Attending management conferences | Facilitating knowledge sharing sessions<br>Conducting workshops |
| Data Scientist        | Taking data science courses<br>Reading analytics books       | Publishing data notebooks<br>Sharing code samples               |


- 4. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">gain experience</mark>

# 7 Collective Systemic Reasoning 
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">systemic reasoning</mark> = art + science of supporting ideas, concepts, recommendations with sound reasons
	- there is no one best way
		- only the best possible solution, under the circumstances, based on what we currently know
	- we propose ideas/actions formally/informally all the time (usually via structure communication)
		- i.e. creating a proposition 
- characteristics of systemic reasoning 
	- it is a tool, practice and mindshift 
	- it provides reasons and rationales - supporting ideas others can evaluate and strengthen
	- involves strengthening the reasons that justify our claims
		- weaving ideas together until they have conceptual integrity 
	- it's proactive, synthesised from diverse knowledge + experience
		- based on valid reasons 
	- it's a method of inquiry 
		- involves exploring, discerning, testing and expanding our knowledge
- **systemic reasoning linearly vs systemically (nonlinearly)**
	- linear approach ⛔️
		- *What just happened? (We react to the “top of the iceberg” event.)* 
		- *Does it work as expected, or is it broken?*
		- *How do I fix it or make it do something else?* 
		- *How long will it take to fix?*
	- nonlinear approach ✅
		- *How do we know, first of all, that it’s a “problem”?* 
			- *What is the impact, now and in the future?* 
		- *A problem for whom?* 
		- *How does this problem compare to the other challenges we are currently facing? Is it core to the system’s purpose?*  
		- *What has been happening over time?* 
		- *What are the structures or processes that make it keep happening?* 
		- *Is there a shift in our mental models required to alleviate this problem, rather than patch it?*
- systemic reasoning is collective
	- when done collectively, we integrate + synthesise diverse POVs
	- it is not changing peoples minds - everyone must change their own
		- all you can do is offer a sound, well-reasoned POV
## 7.1 Creating a Proposition
- creating a proposition is systemic reasoning
	- since it includes the reasons that support your proposition 
		- creating a proposition allows you to detangle your thinking
	- proposition has 3 key components
		- premise = idea, action, theory you propose
		- 3-5 reliable, relevant, sound and cogent reasons to justify your premise
		- why the idea is highly impactful and matters right now

![[Screenshot 2025-03-30 at 11.24.26 am.webp| center | 500]]

- practice of building a proposition is simple 
	- **1. write down your idea, action, recommendation, theory**
		- be as clear and concise as possible here 
	- **2. write 3-5 reasons to support it + what convinced you**
		- include direct connection to system's purpose i..e why does this matter now
		- can discover reasons via:
			- bringing together knowledge/experience that led you to this
			- integrate what other people have said that is relevant to your claim 
			- remembering that your goal is to construct a sound proposition, not power hunger
	- **3. strengthen your reasons + their relationships until they are cogent (clear, logical, convincing)**
		- work w others to do this
		- 4 qualities to strengthen your reasons
			- *reliable* = are the reasons true? research or experimentation can back it
			- *relevant* = are the reasons relevant to the circumstances? why is that relevant now
			- *cohesive* = do the reasons work together to form a convincing whole? are they well connected to the proposition and each other? do they sound logical when read aloud
			- *cogent* = can you make your thinking more clear and convincing? if all your reasons are true, claim is probable also true - key is have you considered your proposition from multiple POV?
	- **4. describe any known negative consequences, valid challenges to current thinking**
		- there are almost aways other options
		- why didn't you choose those?
- the quality of what we build == quality of our reasoning 
	- i.e. test coverage for ideas 
	- we use systemic reasoning to structure relationships among possible ways to perceive a situation
		- we integrate info available to us on how the system operates
	- there is no right answer... but there are answers that are sound 
## 7.2 Top-Down Elaboration (One Page TDE)
- using a TDE is like MVP for proposition, consists of 6 sections
	- sections include: Summary; WHY; WHAT; WHO; HOW; WHEN;
	- each has an example I got GPT to write for my JurEE idea
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">1. summary</span>
	- summarises the context + later section, give background if needed
	- use a single paragraph to distil just the main points 

> **Summary**  
   We propose to integrate a suite of small, fine-tuned models as guardrails within our large chatbot architecture. This approach will ensure safe, compliant outputs and high conversation quality while significantly reducing computational costs compared to scaling up with additional large models.

- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">2. why</span>
	- why does this matter to the system as a whole? or the system's primary purpose
	- rule of thumb - everyone who reads the WHY should understand the value
	- explain the underlying value and purpose of the initiative

> **WHY**  
>  The large chatbot system is critical to our operations, but using additional large models for safety and quality controls is both expensive and inefficient. By leveraging small, fine-tuned models, we can:
 >	Reduce overall computational and financial overhead 
 >	Enhance safety by filtering out inappropriate or unsafe responses more efficiently
 >	Maintain high responsiveness and user satisfaction through specialised, agile guardrail components

- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">3. what </span>
	- what exactly will we do? 

>  **WHAT**  
>  We will develop and deploy a set of small models, each fine-tuned for specific guardrail functions, such as:
 >	Detecting and filtering unsafe or biased content 
 >	Ensuring compliance with regulatory and ethical standards
 >	Validating and enhancing the quality of responses from the large chatbot model

- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">4. who </span>
	- who is impacted + how do they stand to benefit? how will you measure it?
	- who is doing the work?

>  **WHO**  
>  Our AI research and engineering teams will design and implement the new architecture. The safety and compliance teams will provide critical input on the guardrail criteria. Early iterations will be trialed with internal users and select beta customers to ensure the models meet performance expectations.

- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">5. how </span>
	- HOW will we do, build, design or continue to explore the WHAT?
		- need to make strong conceptual bridge between WHAT and HOW 
	- if there are alternatives, describe them 

>  **HOW**  
>  The large chatbot model will generate candidate responses which are then evaluated by the fine-tuned guardrail models. A feedback loop will be established, allowing guardrail outputs to influence real-time adjustments and improvements. Regular evaluations and fine-tuning sessions will be scheduled based on performance metrics and user feedback.

- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">6. when </span>
	- need not be precise, but helps ahead of time 

>  **WHEN**  
>  Phase 1 (3 months): Develop and integrate the initial guardrail models, and complete internal testing.
>  Phase 2 (next 3 months): Iterate based on testing feedback and extend trials to a broader beta group.
>  Full Deployment: Aim for a system-wide rollout within 6 to 9 months, contingent on successful validation of performance and safety criteria.

## 7.3 Strengthening the Reasons
- reasons at first will often be ⛔️:
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">wrong</span> - not true or accurate
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">weakly connected</span> - reasons not interrelated 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">disconnected from context</span> 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">biased</span> - including ideas you like, none that you don't 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">opinionated</span> - no emotional overtones, not disrespectful or condescending 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">insufficient</span> - limited POV (your own)
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">vague</span> - not well connected + don't follow natural order 
- as long as you stay open to learning - seeing, clarifying, understanding + discovering as you go 
	- you will improve 
- to **improve your reasons**, make them more: 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">Understandable</span>: well written 
		- good writing is more impactful than bad/sloppy writing 
		- avoid jargon - define any words that don't have specific well understood meaning
		- edit edit edit - partner with someone good at giving editorial feedback 
		- check your grammar 
		- model your proposition - put first sentence of each paragraph on sticky notes in order, still make sense?
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">Reliable</span>: true and justified 
		- sufficient research to ensure what you are saying is true 
		- unless everyone involved has same expertise, justify anything you assert
		- give relevant examples - when something critical to proposition is said to be true, give an example
		- quotes are valuable, use them to support reasoning
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">Relevant</span>: well connected to each other and the context 
		- include the reason your proposition has high value impact to the system as a whole + why now
		- describe situation from cross-functional POV - for tech proposal, what's the business value
		- eliminate reasons with no substance and generalisations 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">Cohesive</span>: flows as a united whole 
		- does each reason/explanation add to + extend the previous one?
		- are there clear bridges between the proposition + each of the reasons?
		- get feedback from others who understand the circumstances from different POV
			- your reasons may be valid but tiny in comparison to other forces impacting the situation 
		- look for logical fallacies + cognitive biases 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">Cogent</span>: convincing
		- put your reasons in natural order, do they read and make sense at conclusion
		- ask yourself - "if all my reasons are true, how likely is my proposition is wrong"
			- ideally the answer is "unlikely"
		- put the idea away for a few days, come back and read it aloud. Does it still work?
		- read it to someone outside the circumstances, does it still make sense?
# 8 Designing Feedback Loops 
- healthy feedback loops accomplish important things
	- give you feedback to improve your systemic reasoning
	- provide useful feedback 
	- always involve consent - not telling others what they should think, but showing them supporting reasoning + evidence
	- enable learning + knowledge flow
- to build conceptual bridges - need to be aware of logical fallacies
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">POV blindness</span> - different people conceptualise ecosystems differently 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">specialised language barriers</span> - define essential words so no ambiguity
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">resistance</span> - people who don't want change or have other motives
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 136, 0)">lack of structured transparency</span> - needs to be able to be modelled by cross functional group 
- how to bridge those
	- before solving for it, **define the problem** 
		- why is it valuable to solve, does everyone agree on this
	- avoid curse of knowledge, **define the words you use**
	- **gather others' expertise**
		- understand + articulate why they view the circumstance differently
	- decide your goal is not be right, but to be **correct under the circumstances**
	- **share thinking + discussions** by making artefacts
		- structure a digital space for these
## 8.1 Asking for Feedback
- some questions to ask the person giving feedback
	- *Have I sufficiently understood the circumstances? What am I missing?* 
	- *Is what I’m saying factually correct?* 
	- *Did anything confuse you? Did you trip on anything?* 
	- *What is the point, as you understood it?* 
		- ask them to repeat your conclusions - can be very humbling
	- *Does the conclusion make sense to you?* 
		- not the same as "do you agree" - higher value goal is to ensure coherent thinking before unpacking w others
- feedback from the right people 
	- people who can edit the writing
	- SMEs
	- other POVs who experience the impact but not involved in the same way
	- people who are impacted
	- the intended audience
	- people who will pay for it 
- golden rule of feedback = "take what you need + leave the rest"
	- If someone you rely on for feedback only gives you critical feedback, no matter what you improve, that person’s feedback is inherently unreliable regardless of their level of expertise
	- they can’t, as they say, see the forest for the trees. Find someone who can
# 9 Pattern Thinking
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">pattern thinking</mark> = “Has this event happened before, and were the circumstances similar?”
	- when looking at a pattern, you are mentally isolating it from the complexity it operates in
	- tips
		- be careful not to imagine patterns only along linear timelines 
		- patterns can also subtly change as the repeat 
		- when noticed, understand the reinforcing ways it caused that
- patterns produce events 
	- as relational complexity increase, our command/control approaches become noisier (not more signal)
	- an simple brief example 
		- for more insidious examples, you might need to look deeper in the iceberg 

> [!example]
> "There's a bug in production"
> - the bug = syntax error a linter would have caught but team has been extra busy, pushing changes faster than usual 
> - your team is missing little things due to less time for peer review so you add a linter to the merge process
> - this decreases the bugs for a little while
> 
> This is an example of pattern thinking
> - rather than viewing each bug as it's own discrete event + reacting to it, you see a pattern
> 	- a repeating event with a common feature (syntax errors)
> - you see circumstances that are encouraging it
> 	- you intervene with relatively small change that improves system as a whole

- figuring out the root cause of recurring outages **requires deep knowledge and experience with the software system**
	- we use pattern thinking to help us decide which forces give the most impact on observable events
- where to look for patterns 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">relationship to time</span> 
		- do events repeat? if so, when?
		- has the repeating event changed over time?
		- has the frequency changed? 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">relationship to context</span> 
		- does an event happen in one context but not in others? 
			- leverage points in a system depend completely on the context 
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">relationships to other parts</span> 
		- need relational patterns, where people and system are deeply aware of context + self-organising
	- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 247)">relationship to structure</span>
		- always held together by long standing structure + processes
## 9.1 Seven Pattern-thinking Questions
- these questions help identify patterns in a software system
- *1. How does information flow?*
	- how + where is it created, shared, stored + shaped?
	- if it's in motion, is it transformed?
	- does info change as context changes?
	- is this flow monitored? should it be?
- *2. What are the events that happen in the system?*
	- when one thing happens, what happens next?
	- what core activities define the system's purpose?
	- when do these activities happen? what do they change?
- *3. What are the boundaries in the system?*
	- what are the encapsulated parts + why do they exist?
	- do they mirror the domain? 
	- how strong or not are these boundaries?
- *4. What are the building blocks in the system?*
	- are there components, modules, classes or other ways to reuse logic?
	- how do they interrelate and form structures?
- *5. What is the delivery process?*
	- how are priorities set?
	- how is quality ensured?
	- how are decision about what matters + doesn't matter get made?
	- what are the core mental models + structures supporting this?
- *6. How are people organised?*
	- what are the hiring practices?
	- how are teams structured?
	- what groups exist in the org + how do they interact with each other?
	- what are the practices expected from a manager?
- *7. How is discourse structured?*
	- who is involved in decision making?
	- are reasons given for decisions?
	- does info flow across teams? up and down the hierarchy?
	- do teams communicate as peers? do they listen to each other?
# 10 Modelling, Together
- systems thinking does not happen without modelling

- what is modelling?

	- models are broader than this 
		- conversations, framing questions + exploring answers, relations to other models

		- e.g. show higher level view that links to more detailed views 
		- e.g. confluence page to see models, diagrams, prioritised stories + code repos 
			- can also be linked to epics in JIRA, zoom in and out of complexity without being overwhelmed 
- the problem space vs the solution space
	- when a group cannot solve a problem, they are usually solving different problems
	- helps to discern difference between problem space and the solution space
		- value of a solution == how well it solves high impact problem 
		- w/o shared understanding of the problem, no valuable solution 
	- use the 7 patterns questions above and try model each 
# 11 Systems Leadership 
- systems leadership is not ⛔️ :
	- management
	- unique to technology 
	- exclusively subject matter expertise
- what systems leadership is ✅ :
	- building relationships with other experts, when you don't have their skills
	- facilitate a cross-functional communication structure that mirrors a technology system that meets our highest-value goals. 
	- design highly effective knowledge flows in the org
	- synthesise knowledge + experience into reliable/relevant/sound recommendations. 
	- build cross-functional teams that are high-performing 
		- because they think well together and understand the org’s needs
	- discover leverage points in our system and advocate for meaningful, structural change supported by sound reasons
	- design and empower knowledge workers 
		- as a system of learning that generates valuable solutions to the problems we face
- architecting communication design involves
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Communication skills</mark> 
		- using text, code, and visuals to share the thinking behind your ideas
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Systemic reasoning</mark> 
		- crafting ideas that are supported by sound reasons and continuous learning
		- i.e. doing the work required to have a trustworthy opinion. 
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Effective partnership</mark> 
		- improving impact of your ideas by partnering with others with different POVs
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Proactive listening</mark> 
		- asking questions to ensure you understand
		- giving helpful, productive feedback and advice.
	- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Empathy as a technical skill</mark> 
		- we write code with and for people
- the most valuable contribution for a systems leader = **finding leverage points**
## 11.1 Learning Heuristics
- systems leaders often develop heuristics not rules

	- i.e. mental shortcuts to help you remember best practices 
- how to create learning heuristics 
	- what you know is probably your blocker
	- knowledge more valuable than information - flow not stock 
	- devalue your opinion - not knowledge, it's a judgement you've made
	- one perspective is never enough - the more POVs you consider, the more you understand core principles
	- there's always another right way - when considering a challenge, consider options, explain the recommendation - make your discernment visible

	- describe it using ubiquitous language
- ✅ you regularly create conceptual models (alone or collaboratively) and:
	- treat artefact creation as a core technology skill distinct from documentation or diagramming
	- you are developing a diverse portfolio of systems thinking approaches and modelling techniques, 
		- using them improvisation-ally with a common vocabulary
	- you encourage knowledge flow by creating an ecosystem that supports effective knowledge work
## 12.1 Team Practices - How to Measure Systems Thinking Skills
- **improving conceptual integrity for solutions**
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 1</span>: team members describe why a recommendation serves the system’s purpose in a high-priority way
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 2</span>: team members describe multiple cross-functional perspectives on the recommendation
		- including one that disagrees when possible
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 3</span>: team members describe at least one alternative option and why their recommended option is preferable
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 4</span>: team members model system patterns and recommend changes that have a positive impact
		- using the iceberg model whenever possible
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 5</span>: team members collectively improve people processes and understand how to support and reinforce changes in thinking, communication, and behaviour
- **improving knowledge stock**
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 1</span>: whenever team members give advice or share an idea
		- they also share the supporting reasons that are true, relevant, strong, and cohesive
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 2</span>: before responding to others, team members respectfully acknowledge what they’ve heard and understood
		- and work to strengthen the reasons shared, even in disagreement
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 3</span>: team members share and collaboratively create artefacts that capture their thinking, remaining curious and improvisational in their approach
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 4</span>: when external expertise is needed, team members partner with others to think about the problem together
- **improving knowledge flow**
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 1</span>: create consistent experiences that encourage team members to learn together and build sound recommendations
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 2</span>: leverage the skills, experience, and judgment of team members when facing challenges, 
		- synthesise their knowledge into recommendations based on valid reasons
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 3</span>: create relationships among models and other artefacts to cultivate a knowledge system where the team’s thinking and learning drives org success
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 4</span>: approach technology development as a sociotechnical system that adapts to change
		- and when restructuring teams, technology, or processes, describe the changing patterns of thought, action, behaviour, and mental models
	- <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">goal 5</span>: reinforce desired behaviours (especially learning) through feedback loops, 
		- and discourage undesired behaviours (especially derision) through improved structures and feedback loops
