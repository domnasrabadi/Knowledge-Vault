## 0.1 Core Premise
- **Passion is overrated:** the common advice to “follow your passion” is misleading. Most people develop passion _after_ becoming skilled and valuable in their work.
- **Skill before passion:** fulfilling work comes from cultivating rare and valuable skills that give you leverage and autonomy. Passion is the result of mastery, not the starting point.
## 0.2 The Craftsman Mindset
- **Focus on value:** approach your work like a craftsman — prioritise what you can offer, not what the work offers you. Measure success by the quality of value you create, not immediate emotional satisfaction.
- **Relentless improvement:** dedicate yourself to honing rare and valuable skills through deliberate, focused practice. Treat skill development as an ongoing, disciplined pursuit.
## 0.3 Career Capital
- **Definition:** career capital is the collection of rare and valuable skills you acquire over time. The more capital you have, the more control and satisfaction you can command in your career.
- **Acquisition:** identify skills that are both rare and valuable in your field. Use deliberate practice to refine these skills beyond average competency.
- **Leverage:** spend career capital to gain desirable traits like autonomy, creativity and impact in your work.
### 0.3.1 The Four Rules of Career Capital
1. **Don’t follow your passion:** begin by building skills; passion will emerge naturally as you grow in competence and influence.
2. **Be so good they can’t ignore you:** develop rare skills to a level where they set you apart and create unique opportunities.
3. **Turn down promotions (sometimes):** avoid career moves that increase responsibility but reduce your ability to build career capital.
4. **Mission comes after mastery:** only pursue a bold mission or purpose after you’ve achieved enough mastery to execute it effectively.
## 0.4 Control and Autonomy
- **Why control matters:** greater control over your work — how, when and on what you work — is a key driver of job satisfaction.
- **Earned control:** attempting to claim autonomy without sufficient career capital often leads to failure. Build expertise first, then negotiate for control.
## 0.5 The Role of Deliberate Practice
- **Continuous challenge:** work at the edge of your current abilities to stimulate growth.
- **Constant feedback:** seek feedback to identify weaknesses and correct them.
- **Structured effort:** break down skills into smaller components for targeted improvement and avoid “busyness” that doesn’t enhance skill mastery.
## 0.6 The Importance of Mission
- **Mission as a unifying focus:** a compelling mission can guide your career once you have mastery. Missions often emerge from deep engagement in a specific field, not from day‑one clarity.
- **Small bets:** test ideas through small, low‑risk projects before committing fully. Let success guide you toward a scalable, long‑term mission.
## 0.7 The Law of Financial Viability
- **Check for market demand:** a mission is more sustainable when it aligns with economic value. Don’t invest deeply in a mission unless you can see a realistic path for it to support you.
## 0.8 Final Insight
Passion is cultivated, not discovered. Start by becoming excellent at something rare and valuable. Use your excellence to gain control, craft a mission and ultimately build work you love.
