# 1 **First-Principle & Logical Models**
- **First-Principles Thinking:** Reduce a problem to undeniable facts, discard assumptions, and reason upward from fundamentals.
- **Inversion:** Define the opposite of your goal and design safeguards that eliminate those failure paths.
- **Occam’s Razor:** Prefer the simplest explanation that fully fits the facts unless extra complexity is justified.
- **Second-Order Thinking:** Forecast not just the immediate outcome but the knock-on effects before acting.
- **Falsification:** Strength-test ideas by actively seeking evidence that could prove them wrong.
- **Chesterton’s Fence:** Understand the original purpose of an existing rule or structure before altering or removing it.
- **The Map Is Not the Territory:** Remember that models/labels are simplified representations, not reality itself.
# 2 **Probabilistic & Statistical Models**
- **Bayes’ Theorem:** Incrementally update belief probabilities as new, relevant evidence appears.
- **Expected Value:** Multiply each possible outcome by its probability and choose the option with the highest total.
- **Regression to the Mean:** Expect extreme performances or events to be followed by more ordinary ones.
- **Power Law / Pareto Principle:** Identify and double down on the small fraction of inputs that deliver most of the results.
- **Black-Swan Awareness:** Prepare for rare, high-impact events that can dominate long-term outcomes.
- **Survivorship Bias:** Include invisible failures, not just visible successes, when drawing conclusions.
# 3 **Systems & Complexity Models**
- **Feedback Loops:** Map reinforcing loops (which amplify change) and balancing loops (which stabilise systems).
- **Leverage Points:** Apply effort where a small tweak yields disproportionately large system-wide effect.
- **Constraints / Bottlenecks:** Improve overall throughput by relieving the slowest or scarcest step.
- **Stocks & Flows:** Track what accumulates (stocks) and what moves (flows) to understand dynamics over time.
- **Compounding:** Reinvest gains consistently to produce accelerating returns over long horizons.
# 4 **Economic & Incentive Models**
- **Opportunity Cost:** Value a choice by the best alternative you give up, not just its sticker price.
- **Incentive-Caused Bias:** Recognise that behaviour follows the reward structure—yours and others’.
- **Principal-Agent Problem:** Align incentives and add transparency when the agent has different goals than the principal.
- **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Quantify all significant (tangible and intangible) costs and benefits before deciding.
- **Supply, Demand & Elasticity:** Predict quantity and price shifts by understanding responsiveness of buyers/sellers.
- **Moral Hazard:** Monitor situations where protection from downside encourages excessive risk-taking.
# 5 **Psychological & Cognitive Models**
- **System 1 vs. System 2 Thinking:** Switch from fast intuition (System 1) to slower, analytical thinking (System 2) when stakes or novelty are high.
- **Availability Bias:** Counter reliance on vivid or recent examples by checking base rates.
- **Anchoring:** Set your own reference point before exposure to external numbers or opinions.
- **Loss Aversion:** Recognise that losses loom larger than gains and frame choices accordingly.
- **Social Proof:** Validate group behaviour with data before copying, especially under uncertainty.
- **Hanlon’s Razor:** Attribute undesirable actions to error or incompetence before assuming malice.
# 6 **Process & Execution Models**
- **OODA Loop:** Iterate through Observe → Orient → Decide → Act faster than changing conditions or competitors.
- **Checklist Discipline:** Externalise routine steps in checklists to reduce preventable errors.
- **Eisenhower Matrix:** Schedule important-but-not-urgent tasks before they become crises.
- **5 Whys:** Reach the root cause of a problem by asking “why?” successively until a process flaw is exposed.
# 7 **Strategic & Game-Theoretic Models**
- **Circle of Competence:** Operate primarily within domains you genuinely understand; expand cautiously.
- **Prisoner’s Dilemma:** Design rules or trust mechanisms that make cooperation more rewarding than defection.
- **Nash Equilibrium:** Anticipate situations where no participant can improve their position by unilaterally changing strategy.
- **Optionality:** Create low-cost paths that offer significant upside while limiting downside exposure.
# 8 **Learning & Meta-Cognition Models**
- **Feynman Technique:** Teach a concept in simple language to expose and repair gaps in understanding.
- **Deliberate Practice:** Isolate sub-skills, push just beyond comfort, and seek immediate feedback for rapid improvement.
- **Spaced Repetition:** Review information at increasing intervals just before it would otherwise fade.
- **Transfer of Learning:** Map structural similarities so skills from one domain help solve problems in another.
- **Latticework of Mental Models:** Combine perspectives from multiple disciplines to reach more robust conclusions.
