### 0.1.1 **Core Philosophy of GTD**
- **Mind Like Water:**
    - True productivity comes from a calm, clear mind that responds appropriately to incoming demands.
    - The goal isn’t doing more; it’s creating mental space for focus and creativity.
- **Capture Everything:**
    - Keep nothing floating in your head—write down every idea, task, or commitment.
    - An uncluttered mind frees energy for problem-solving and creative work.
### 0.1.2 **The Five Steps of Mastering Workflow**
- **Capture:**
    - Collect everything that has your attention into an external, trusted system (inboxes—physical or digital, notebooks, apps).
- **Clarify:**
    - Process each captured item:
        - If **not actionable**: trash it, file it for reference, or add it to a “Someday/Maybe” list.
        - If **actionable**: determine the very next physical, visible step (Next Action).
- **Organize:**
    - Sort actionable items into appropriate lists:
        - **Next Actions:** specific, visible next steps.
        - **Projects:** multi-step desired outcomes requiring tracking.
        - **Waiting For:** delegated or pending others’ actions.
        - **Calendar:** time-specific commitments.
- **Reflect:**
    - Regularly review your system, especially via the **Weekly Review**:
        - Clear inboxes.
        - Update lists.
        - Reconnect with priorities.
- **Engage:**
    - Choose what to do based on:
        - Context (tools, location, people available)
        - Time available
        - Energy available
        - Priority
### 0.1.3 **The Two-Minute Rule**
- If a task takes less than two minutes, do it immediately.
- Prevents small tasks from accumulating and creating mental clutter.
### 0.1.4 **Defining Projects and Outcomes**
- A **project** is any desired outcome requiring more than one action step.
- Clearly define the **successful outcome** before starting.
- Break large projects into concrete next actions to maintain momentum.
### 0.1.5 **Contexts and Action Lists**
- **Context-Based Organization:**
    - Group tasks by where/how they can be done (e.g., @Office, @Home, @Computer, @Calls).
- **Benefits:**
    - Reduces decision fatigue.
    - Makes action choices fast and intuitive.
### 0.1.6 **Managing Someday/Maybe Lists**
- Store ideas, aspirations, and non-urgent goals for future review.
- Keeps you free from pressure while keeping opportunities visible.
### 0.1.7 **The Weekly Review**
- **Purpose:**
    - Keep your system current, complete, and trustworthy.
- **Process:**
    - Empty all inboxes.
    - Review calendars (past and upcoming).
    - Update projects and next actions.
    - Revisit Someday/Maybe for possible activation.
- **Result:**
    - Confidence that nothing important is slipping through the cracks.
### 0.1.8 **Maintaining a Trusted System**
- Your system must be:
    - **Complete:** Everything that matters is captured and tracked.
    - **Current:** Outdated items are cleared; active items are accurate.
    - **Accessible:** Easy to update and use daily.
### 0.1.9 **Mental Clarity and Productivity**
- **Why It Works:**
    - Reduces cognitive load by eliminating the need to remember everything.
    - Creates mental trust so your brain can focus on doing rather than tracking.
- **Outcome:**
    - Stress-free productivity driven by clarity, control, and perspective.
