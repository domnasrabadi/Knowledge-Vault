One entry per concept or entity for the whole vault. Every name in every note is resolved to an entry, so the graph counts one thing once, whatever it was called. Each entry is a file in 50 Graph/Registry/; you can edit its aliases and definition there. New names start as pending and are usable straight away until you approve, merge or reject them.

What is a concept?

A concept is an idea that many sources can talk about: a method, a problem, a property or a pattern. It has a definition, not a maker. An entity is one particular named thing. A claim is a statement about them that you could agree or disagree with.

Quick test: can you define it without naming one product, person or paper? Concept. Is it one specific thing that someone made or is? Entity. Could it be true or false? Claim.

Co-occurrence, and how it makes communities

  1. Co-occur means the same independence group (a note, or a whole Book) mentions both entries.
  2. Counted across the whole vault, NPMI asks whether two entries appear together more often than chance, given how common each one is. More than chance gives an edge, weighted by how much more. A pair needs at least 2 groups in common.
  3. A hub such as “LLM” appears with nearly everything, so appearing with it says little: its NPMI is near zero and its edges are weak (dotted).
  4. Leiden clustering then looks for sets of entries joined by many strong edges and few edges to the rest. Each set is a community, and P6 writes its summary from the community's best-supported claims.

Why have communities? For global questions (“what themes run through everything on X?”) they give coverage across every cluster, instead of the 30 passages most similar to the question, which tend to come from a few notes. They also stand in for a hand-built topic hierarchy and drive topic-tag suggestions. They steer retrieval but are never cited.

Two pipelines

The graph is built ahead of time; questions only read it

All the expensive LLM work (finding mentions, writing and matching claims) happens at build time, once per new or changed note. Asking a question reads the finished graph: several searches, a few LLM calls, then an answer.

Build time per new or changed note

  1. 1
    Parse and chunk

    Read the frontmatter, then split on headings, then bullets, then sentences, so no passage exceeds 400 tokens.

  2. 2
    Annotate, embed and index each passage

    Every passage gets all three, straight after chunking:

    • 2a

      Annotate: heading breadcrumb, position, token count, voice, highlight density.

    • 2b

      Embed: one vector per passage, for semantic search.

    • 2c

      Index: added to the BM25 keyword index, for exact-word search.

  3. 3
    Find and resolve mentions

    Two linked steps: find the names, then map each one to the Registry.

    • 3a

      Find · P1: spans of text that name a concept or entity.

    • 3b

      Resolve · P2: exact match against Registry names and aliases first (no LLM). Otherwise the 5 most similar entries by embedding, and P2 decides: auto-link (≥ 0.85), provisional link plus review (0.60–0.85 or unsure), or a new pending entry plus review (new, or below 0.60).

  4. 4
    Write claims · P3

    Standalone claims with ABOUT targets, qualifiers, referenced time, volatility and a confidence; each claim is embedded.

  5. 5
    Match claims · P4

    Against the 10 most similar claims that share a target: same, refines, contradicts or different.

  6. 6
    Score

    w per passage, the best w per independence group, ln(1 + sum): stored support and opposition.

  7. 7
    Graph jobs · P6, P7

    NPMI co-occurrence edges, Leiden communities and their summaries, concept pages.

  8. 8
    Review Queue

    Uncertain merges, matches and contradictions wait for your decision.

Result: a graph of passages, claims, concepts, entities and communities, stored locally, with the LLM outputs exported to JSONL in git.

Question time per question

  1. 1
    Decompose · P9

    Sub-questions, each tagged global, local, temporal or connective, plus any time range.

  2. 2
    Retrieve from every channel

    All of these run for each sub-question:

    • 2a

      Passage vectors: embedding similarity between the question and each passage.

    • 2b

      BM25 keywords: exact-word search over the same passages.

    • 2c

      Claim vectors: embedding similarity over claims.

    • 2d

      Graph expansion: from the concepts and entities the question names, up to 2 hops.

    • 2e

      Community summaries: global sub-questions only; pick communities, take their top claims.

  3. 3
    Fuse with RRF

    Each item scores the sum of 1 / (60 + rank) across channels; duplicates merged; top 30 kept. RRF uses ranks only: whether claim support should also rerank results is an open question.

  4. 4
    Coverage loop · P10

    Anything missing? Call tools (search, expand, find paths, read source) and fuse again, up to 3 rounds.

  5. 5
    Validate citations · P11

    Does each cited passage really support its claim? Drop the ones that don't.

  6. 6
    Apply time

    Recency decay and the contested check, computed for today rather than stored.

  7. 7
    Synthesise · P12

    Answer within a 12,000-token context, following the answer rules.

Result: an answer with citations to notes and passages, evidence labels (support, independent groups, contested or qualified, referenced time), and what the vault doesn't cover.

    Scoring, in detail

    What goes into each passage's weight

    w = quality × highlight × extraction confidence × resolution confidence, then × recency decay at query time.

    Quality

    Stars become a multiplier: unrated 1.0, ⭐️ 1.0, ⭐️⭐️ 1.5, ⭐️⭐️⭐️ 2.0. It raises claim support, not search rank directly.

    Highlight density

    The share of a passage’s characters inside <mark> tags; factor = 1 + density (1.0 to 2.0).

    Vault check: 0 of 173 raw notes have <mark> tags, because a raw note is already your highlights. 54 of 106 structured and 2 of 40 distilled notes have any. The average share is about 1%, so the factor is about 1.0 everywhere. Proposal: drop it, and set voice by note status (raw = highlight, distilled = mine). Pending your decision.

    Extraction confidence

    How sure P3 is that the passage asserts the claim, reported by P3 itself. Self-reported LLM confidence is poorly calibrated: a weak signal, possibly better as high, medium or low.

    Resolution confidence

    How sure the merge into this claim is: P4’s confidence (or P2’s). 1.0 for an exact match, a brand-new claim or anything you reviewed. Fixed at 1.0 in the playground.

    Qualified (½)

    An input flag, not a score. A QUALIFIED_SUPPORT passage backs the claim only under a condition, so it counts at half its w. The condition is shown beside the claim in answers.

    Recency decay

    0.5^(age ÷ half-life), set by volatility. Age runs from the claim’s referenced time (when the claim is about), not from when you read the note. Applied only at query time.

    Two separate dampers

    Best passage per group stops one note with many passages from counting many times. ln(1 + sum) across groups makes each extra independent group add less than the one before. It slows growth but never caps it.

    Question pathways

    Different questions light up different parts of the pipeline

    Pick a question category from the evaluation set. At run time every channel runs for every sub-question, and global ones also use community summaries: the categories are not a router. The highlight shows which channel usually carries the answer, which is what the evaluation set checks.

    Passage vectors

    The question and every passage are embedded; the passages closest in meaning come back, even when the wording differs.

    BM25 keywords

    Classic keyword search over the same passages. It catches exact names, acronyms and rare terms that embeddings blur.

    Claim vectors

    Embedding search over claims instead of passages. More precise, and each hit brings its support, its contradictions and the passages behind it.

    Graph expansion

    Resolve the concepts and entities the question names, then walk outward up to 2 hops (at most 10 neighbours each) along co-occurrence, typed and INSTANCE_OF edges. It collects claims ABOUT what it reaches: up to 5 passages per concept and 3 per source. Hubs are damped by 1 / ln(e + degree). It finds related material that shares neither words nor meaning with the question.

    Community summaries

    Each community has an LLM-written summary with an embedding. For a global question, the summaries closest to it choose the communities; their best-supported claims and passages become the evidence. Summaries steer, and are never cited.

    Fuse (RRF)

    Each channel returns a ranked list. An item’s score is the sum of 1 / (k + its rank) over the channels that found it, with k = 60; duplicates are merged and the top 30 kept. Ranks, not raw scores, so the channels never need normalising.

    Coverage loop (P10)

    Runs every time, but simple questions usually stop after the first check. If a sub-question is unanswered or a link is missing, it calls tools (search_text, resolve_entity, expand, find_paths, read_source) and fuses again, up to 3 rounds. Multi-hop questions are the main case.

    Validate (P11)

    For each claim–passage pair about to be cited: does the passage really support the claim? It answers supports, partial or no, with a reason, and the no pairs are dropped. A faithfulness filter, not a reranker.

    Synthesise (P12)

    The final LLM call, with a 12,000-token budget. Rules: absence means “not in your vault”, never “false”; superseded claims are used only for history questions; summaries and concept pages are never cited. Possible simplification: P9, P10 and P12 could be left to whichever agent you ask from (Claude Code or an MCP client), so only the retrieval tools need building.

    Keeping it current

    A sync touches only what changed, and your decisions go in through markdown

    The build pipeline runs once over the whole vault. After that, notes keep arriving, changing and moving, and a full rebuild would pay for every LLM call again. A sync (phase 6) compares the vault with the last commit it indexed and redoes only what changed. It also applies your Review Queue decisions, which is where entity merges and approvals get settled, and it regenerates anything built from data that changed. Entity resolution is one part of a sync, not all of it.

    One sync run

    1. Apply your decisions from Review Queue.md
    2. Diff in-scope notes from last_indexed_commit to HEAD
      Added or modifiedRe-chunk; unchanged passages keep their IDs; new ones go through P1–P4
      DeletedRemove its passages and their edges
      Frontmatter onlyRecompute weights; no LLM calls
    3. Mark claims left with no support as orphaned
    4. Recompute support, opposition and CO_OCCURS where edges changed
    5. Regenerate stale summaries, pages and caches
    6. Write new review items and reports, export JSONL, advance the commit
